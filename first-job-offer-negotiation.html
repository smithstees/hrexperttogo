What to expect

What a first job offer review covers

The point is not to turn a first offer into a fight. It is to understand the full picture, make an informed decision, and have a clear, professional conversation if a question or request makes sense. See also the broader salary negotiation coaching service for support beyond this decision moment.

What we cover

  • Base salary in context, including how it compares with the role, level, and location
  • Benefits and practical tradeoffs that may matter as much as salary
  • A first job offer negotiation strategy that fits the situation
  • Non-salary levers, such as start date, title, flexibility, or development support
  • Timing and response wording, including how to ask without overexplaining
  • Sign-on bonus and PTO questions when those items are part of the offer

What this is not

  • Negotiating with an employer for the graduate
  • Legal, tax, investment, or financial advice
  • A guarantee that an employer will move on salary or any other term
  • Pressure to negotiate when the facts do not support it
Is this right for your student

A job offer review for new graduates is most useful before an answer goes back to the employer. Parents sometimes ask, should my college graduate negotiate salary? The useful answer comes from the actual offer, the role, and the graduate's priorities.

Common first-offer questions we get from parents

Should my graduate negotiate their first offer?

Sometimes, yes. The best answer depends on the role, the offer, the process so far, and what the graduate can support with a clear request.

How much room is there really?

Entry-level ranges can be tight. We look at the specifics instead of assuming there is unlimited room or none at all.

What if they lose the offer?

A professional, well-timed question is different from an ultimatum. We talk through the risk and help the graduate choose an approach they can stand behind.

When should we book?

As soon as the offer arrives. A session before the response deadline creates room to think rather than rush.
