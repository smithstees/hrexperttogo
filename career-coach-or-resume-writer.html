For College Seniors and New Grads

Career Coach, Resume Writer, or Something Else? What a Graduating Senior Actually Needs

By Ty Smith, SHRM-CP | HR Expert to go | Job Search

You graduate in the spring. You know you need to start a real job search, and you are willing to pay for help. But when you start looking, everyone seems to be a career coach, a resume writer, a career counselor, or some mix of all three. It is hard to tell what any of them actually do, or which one you need.

The right answer depends less on the title and more on the problem you are trying to solve. Start there and the choice gets much easier.

What Each One Actually Does

Resume writer

A resume writer creates or rewrites your resume for you. You share your background, and they produce the document. Some also write cover letters or professional profiles.

This can make sense if you already know exactly what kind of job you want, your experience clearly fits it, and what you need is a polished document. The tradeoff is that you will have to explain every line in an interview, including lines you did not write. A resume writer usually will not help you decide what to apply for, run your search, or prepare for interviews.

Career coach

A career coach works with you on the search itself. That can include figuring out which roles fit, reviewing how your resume and profile read to employers, building a search plan, practicing interviews, and thinking through an offer. With a coach, you usually do the work yourself with their feedback and guidance.

Coaching makes sense when the problem is bigger than one document: you are not sure what to target, you are applying and hearing nothing, or you are getting interviews but not offers.

Campus career counselor

Most colleges have a career center with counselors who help students explore options, review resumes, and practice interviews. While you are still enrolled, this help is usually free, and many schools keep it available for a while after graduation. If you have not used yours yet, start there before you pay anyone.

The limitation is time. Career centers serve a lot of students, so appointments can be short, and senior year is their busiest season.

Mentors and people in your field

Professors, alumni, former supervisors, and people who already do the job you want can tell you what the work is really like and who is hiring. They are not a replacement for structured help, but they are one of the most valuable parts of any search.

Start With the Problem, Not the Title

Here is a simple way to match what you are struggling with to the kind of help that fits.

  • "I don't know what jobs fit my degree." You need direction first. Start with your campus career center or a career coach. A resume writer cannot write toward a target you have not chosen.
  • "I don't know how to run a job search." You need a strategy: where to look, how many applications make sense, and how to use your network. That is coaching work.
  • "I'm applying everywhere and hearing nothing." Something in your resume, your targets, or both is not landing. A coach can help you find out which one it is. A new document alone may not fix a targeting problem.
  • "I get interviews but no offers." Your materials are working. Your interviews need practice and honest feedback.
  • "I know exactly what I want and just need a polished resume." A resume writer may be the right fit, as long as you review every line and can explain it.

Many seniors have more than one of these problems. That is normal. The goal is to work on the one that is holding you back the most right now.

Questions to Ask Before You Pay Anyone

  • What will you do, and what will I do?
  • Have you worked in hiring, HR, or recruiting? Have you actually read resumes and made hiring decisions?
  • What will I leave each session with?
  • What exactly is included in the price, and how long do I have to use it?
  • Do you work with college students and new graduates, or mostly experienced professionals?

Be careful with anyone who guarantees you a job or a specific salary. No coach, writer, or counselor controls who an employer hires.

The Bottom Line

You do not need to figure out the right title before you get help. Figure out the problem first. If you need a target or a plan, start with your career center or a coach. If you know your target and need a polished document, a resume writer may fit. If you are applying and hearing nothing, or interviewing without offers, get honest feedback from someone who has done the hiring.
