What to expect

What career coaching for college students actually is

This is focused, one-to-one help with the decisions and skills that come before a first real job. It can work as a focused session or as part of a broader plan, depending on what your student needs. Explore the job search strategy, resume coaching, and interview preparation services for focused support.

What we cover

  • Direction around a major, first role, or realistic starting point
  • A resume that connects coursework, internships, projects, and strengths to the roles being pursued
  • Interview preparation that sounds like the student, not a memorized script
  • A first job offer review, including questions to ask and how to respond
  • A first-90-days plan for starting well after the offer is accepted

What this is not

  • A resume writing service that sends back a document with no discussion
  • Job placement or an introduction to an employer
  • Therapy, counseling, or clinical support
  • A guaranteed placement, because no coach can promise that
Is this right for your student

Parents often arrive with a simple question: do I need a career coach for college students, or am I looking for career counseling for college students? You may be wondering whether a career coach for my son or a career coach for my daughter could help my college student get a job. The answer depends on the immediate challenge, and the cards below make that easier to sort out.

Is this right for your student

Choosing a major or first role

Your student has options but no clear way to decide what fits now, or what a sensible first role could look like.

Actively job searching and stuck

Applications are going out, but the process is scattered, slow, or not producing conversations.

Interview coming up soon

A real interview is on the calendar and your student wants to prepare without sounding rehearsed.

Offer in hand and unsure

There is an offer to consider, questions about salary or benefits, and a response deadline. See the salary negotiation and offer review page for more detail.

A practical partner for parents

Why parents choose HR Expert to go

An outside perspective

Your student may need to hear practical feedback from someone outside the family. Coaching creates a place to ask questions, test ideas, and make a plan without adding pressure to the kitchen-table conversation.

HR-side experience

Ty Smith is a SHRM-certified HR professional with 20+ years of experience in HR leadership and consulting. The focus is on helping a new graduate understand how employers read materials, interviews, and offers.

A structured plan, not a wish

Parents are often already encouraging their student. A coaching plan adds a sequence: decide what to target, prepare the materials, practice the conversation, and handle the next decision. It is concrete support, not vague motivation.
